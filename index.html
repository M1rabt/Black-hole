A short guide to black holes

A boundary, not a surface.

A black hole is a region where gravity is so strong that, past a boundary called the event horizon, nothing can escape—not even light.

01 / BOUNDARY

Event horizon

The event horizon marks the point of no return. It is not a solid shell; it is the boundary around a black hole beyond which every possible path forward leads inward.

02 / CORE

Singularity

In classical general relativity, matter collapsing into a black hole leads toward a singularity, where the theory predicts extreme density. What truly happens there remains an open question in physics.

03 / INFALLING MATTER

Accretion disk

Gas and dust orbiting toward a black hole can form a hot, bright disk. Friction and compression heat this material, so the disk can glow before matter crosses the event horizon.

04 / BENT LIGHT

Gravitational lensing

Gravity curves the paths of light. Near a black hole, this bending can distort and magnify views of bright matter behind it, sometimes making parts of the disk appear wrapped around the darkness.

About this visualization. This interactive scene is an artistic illustration, not a numerical relativity or astrophysics model. The mass and rotation controls change visual parameters only; the readouts are settings, not measurements or predictions.
